Official Guide

How to Get Organic Certification in India: A Complete Step-by-Step Guide

Navigate the regulatory landscape of NPOP and PGS-India with this comprehensive educational resource for farmers, FPOs, and agribusinesses.

Reading time: 25 mins Last updated: November 17, 2026 Regulatory Fact-Checked

1. Introduction

Organic certification is a comprehensive verification process that ensures agricultural products are grown, handled, and processed according to established national and international standards. For Indian producers, certification is the bridge between traditional chemical-free farming and the premium organic marketplace.

Certification provides third-party assurance to buyers that the "Organic" label on a product is backed by rigorous inspections and full traceability. It builds trust, allows access to high-value export markets, and ensures that the integrity of organic production is maintained from farm to fork.

Crucial Distinction:

Following organic farming practices (adopting organic methods) does not automatically mean that a product is certified organic. Certification is a separate legal and administrative process required for commercial labeling and export.

2. What is Organic Certification in India?

In India, organic certification is governed by national standards that define everything from seed selection to soil management. The process involves:

  • Standard Compliance: Adhering to the National Programme for Organic Production (NPOP) or Participatory Guarantee System (PGS-India) standards.
  • Verification: Systematic audits of farm inputs, management practices, and site history.
  • Documentation: Maintaining detailed records of every activity on the farm or processing unit.
  • Integrity: Preventing contamination from conventional farms and ensuring no mixing of products during storage or transport.

3. Types of Organic Certification in India

India operates two distinct certification systems. Understanding the difference is vital for choosing the right path for your business.

Feature NPOP (National Programme for Organic Production) PGS-India (Participatory Guarantee System)
Implementing Authority APEDA, Ministry of Commerce & Industry NCOF, Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare
Verification Method Third-party audits by accredited Certification Bodies (CBs). Peer-to-peer appraisal within Local Groups of farmers.
Primary Market Required for Export and accepted for domestic sale. Exclusively for the Domestic Indian Market.
Structure Individual or Large Grower Groups (with ICS). Local Groups (min 5 farmers) or Individuals.
Traceability TraceNet (Electronic system). PGS-India Portal.
Logos India Organic Logo. PGS-India Organic / Green Logo.

4. Who Can Apply?

Certification is available to various stakeholders in the supply chain:

  • Individual Farmers: Owners or lessees of agricultural land.
  • Grower Groups: Organized groups of farmers (often marginal/small) in the same geographical area.
  • FPOs & Cooperatives: Legal entities representing farmers for collective certification.
  • Processors: Units handling, cleaning, grinding, or packaging organic products.
  • Traders & Exporters: Entities buying and selling certified products without changing their nature.

5. NPOP vs PGS-India: Which Should You Choose?

Choose NPOP If:

  • You plan to export products to the EU, USA, or other countries.
  • You are a large commercial farm or a well-funded FPO.
  • You target high-end retail chains and institutional buyers.
  • You are a processor/manufacturer using NPOP raw materials.

Choose PGS-India If:

  • You are a small/marginal farmer selling locally.
  • You sell directly to consumers, farmers' markets, or local stores.
  • You want a low-cost, community-driven certification system.
  • You have no immediate plans for international export.

6. Eligibility and Preparation

Successful certification begins long before the inspector arrives. Key preparation steps include:

  • Land Records: Clear ownership or lease deeds for the past 3 years.
  • Conversion Start: The period starts from the date of first application or registration.
  • Buffer Zones: Creating 5-10 meter barriers (e.g., live fences) to prevent chemical drift from neighbors.
  • Equipment: Ensuring sprayers and tools used for conventional farming are not mixed with organic tools.
  • Input Verification: Checking the "Prohibited List" in NPOP Annex 1 or PGS Standards.

7. Documents Required

Documentation requirements vary by operator type. Below are the standard checklists:

Farmer Checklist

  • Application form & Agreement
  • Identity & Address proof
  • Land document (7/12 extract / RTC)
  • Detailed Farm Map (Hand-drawn or GPS)
  • Organic System Plan (OSP)
  • Farm Diary (Last 3 years history)

Processor Checklist

  • Processing unit layout & Plan
  • Product flow chart (Step-by-step)
  • Raw material purchase records
  • Cleaning & Sanitation logs
  • Packaging & Labeling samples
  • FSSAI License / Registration

8. NPOP Certification: Step-by-Step

1

Select a Certification Body (CB)

Choose from the list of APEDA-accredited CBs. Contact multiple bodies to compare their fee structures and service areas.

2

Application & Registration

Submit the application form and pay the registration fee. The CB will register you on TraceNet, APEDA's online portal.

3

Organic System Plan (OSP) Submission

Submit a detailed document describing your farm management, inputs, and contamination prevention measures.

4

Document Review & Inspection

The CB reviews your documents. Once approved, an inspector visits your farm for an onsite verification, usually once a year.

5

Certification Decision

The inspection report is reviewed by a certification committee. If compliant, a Scope Certificate is issued.

9. NPOP Grower Group Certification and ICS

Grower groups are essential for small farmers to share certification costs. The cornerstone of group certification is the Internal Control System (ICS).

ICS Requirements:

  • Internal Inspector: A person from the group who inspects 100% of member farms twice a year.
  • ICS Manager: Responsible for overall documentation and compliance.
  • Internal Approval Committee (IAC): Makes decisions on non-compliances within the group.
  • Group Size: Generally min 25 to max 500 members under NPOP 2024 revisions (check specific CB rules).

10. PGS-India Certification: Step-by-Step

PGS is based on trust and peer-oversight. The process is managed through Local Groups (LG).

  1. Form a Local Group: Minimum 5 farmers from the same village or vicinity.
  2. Registration: Register the LG on the PGS-India portal and select a Regional Council (RC).
  3. Peer Appraisal: Farmers within the group inspect each other's farms using a checklist.
  4. Data Entry: The group uploads peer appraisal summaries and farm details to the portal.
  5. RC Verification: The Regional Council verifies the data and may conduct random physical checks.
  6. Certificate Issuance: Once approved, the LG can download certificates from the portal.

11. Organic Conversion Period

The conversion period is the time it takes for soil and products to be recognized as organic.

Crop Type NPOP Period PGS-India Status
Annual Crops (Grains, Pulses) Min 2 years before sowing. Year 1: In-Conversion (C1)
Year 2: In-Conversion (C2)
Year 3: Organic
Perennial Crops (Fruit trees, Plantation) Min 3 years before first organic harvest. Same 3-year progression as above.

12. Inspection and Non-Compliance

Inspectors check for residues of prohibited chemicals through soil, water, and leaf testing if risk is suspected. Common non-compliances include:

  • Use of treated seeds.
  • Lack of buffer zones.
  • Incomplete farm diary records.
  • Mixing of organic and non-organic harvest.

Sanctions: Range from simple corrective actions to suspension or permanent cancellation of the organic certificate.

13. Certification Cost

Costs are not fixed and depend on several factors:

  • Registration Fee: Usually ₹2,000 - ₹5,000.
  • Inspection Fee: ₹5,000 - ₹15,000 per day plus travel/stay for the inspector.
  • Testing Fee: Lab residue tests (if required) cost ₹5,000 - ₹8,000 per sample.
  • Group Savings: Group certification can reduce the cost per farmer to as low as ₹500 - ₹1,500 per year.

14. FSSAI and Jaivik Bharat

FSSAI regulates the labeling and sale of organic food in India. All organic food must carry the Jaivik Bharat logo.

Exemption: Small original producers with an annual turnover of up to ₹12 Lakh are exempt from FSSAI organic certification for direct sales to end consumers, though they must still follow organic standards.

15. Traceability and Record Keeping

Without records, a product cannot be certified. You must maintain:

  • Farm Diary: Daily log of all farm activities.
  • Input Register: Details of all seeds, fertilizers, and biopesticides used.
  • Harvest & Stock Register: Exact yields and storage location.
  • TCs (Transaction Certificates): Required for every sale between certified parties in NPOP.

16. Common Mistakes

  1. Using GMO seeds or treated seeds from conventional sources.
  2. Forgetting to record a spray of bio-fertilizer in the farm diary.
  3. Poor storage where organic products touch bags previously used for chemicals.
  4. Making "Organic" claims on social media without a valid certificate.

17. Government Support and Schemes

Farmers can seek assistance through:

  • PKVY (Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana): Supports PGS-India group certification and clusters.
  • MOVCDNER: Specific for organic value chain development in North Eastern states.
  • State Subsidies: Many states provide 50-75% subsidy on certification fees for small farmers.
